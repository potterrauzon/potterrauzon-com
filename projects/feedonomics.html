Feedonomics

A marketing site moving onto a new design system one page at a time. A few section types, a navy-and-cream palette kept on a short leash, and headlines that do structural work. Enough to hold forty-odd pages together while they were only half changed over.

Designing a page was never the hard part. The hard part was designing something that could arrive a little at a time. You cannot relaunch a site this size in one night, so whatever we built had to look deliberate standing next to the pages it hadn't gotten to yet. We started with a short list of section types: a navy hero, a logo bar, a run of alternating fifty-fifty rows, a dark proof panel, a gradient card to close on. Type and color carry them. One geometric sans does all the work, warm cream stands in for the usual white so the site reads like a magazine instead of a clinic, and peach is held back for one job only, which is the call to action.

Inside all that quiet we let the headlines get loud. Two short lines with the turn in the second, sitting under a small dotted eyebrow that names the section. The writing ended up being a building material. It paces the scroll, it tells a reader where they are, and it gives a dense technical product a little room to breathe. My part was the direction and the information architecture: what the site was made of, and the order in which a reader ought to meet it.
